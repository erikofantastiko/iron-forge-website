# Website content

Two things live here: how to write plainly for the website, and the facts the copy
must stay true to. Read this alongside `brand-voice.md` for any text change.

## Plain English and common terms

These are the two rules that outrank every other style choice.

1. **Plain, understandable English.** Short sentences, one idea each, active voice,
   everyday words. No corporate filler. Write so a first-time visitor gets it on the
   first read.
2. **Common, familiar terms.** Prefer the word most people already know. When a gym
   term is genuinely needed, add a short plain-English explanation the first time it
   appears on a page.

A note on the app versus the website. Inside the app, Iron Forge explains nothing;
serious lifters know the terms. That is an app-only rule. The website talks to a
broader audience, so on the website you may and should explain terms. Keep the
no-hype, no-fluff spine. Just make it welcoming and clear instead of terse.

### Do and don't

| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| "Leverage advanced periodisation methodologies" | "Plan your training and see what's working" |
| "Autoregulated volume management" | "It suggests when to add weight and when to hold back" |
| "Myo-reps, giant sets, cluster protocols" (bare) | "Myo-reps and giant sets, advanced ways to train, logged as they happen" |
| "Posterior chain hypertrophy tracking" | "Track your back, glutes and hamstrings" |

### Small glossary (plain English for when a term is needed)

- **Drop set**: keep going after a hard set by dropping the weight and repping again.
- **Superset**: two exercises back to back with no rest between them.
- **Myo-reps**: one hard set, then short bursts of a few reps with brief rests.
- **Giant set**: several exercises in a row as one round.
- **HRV (heart rate variability)**: a heart-rate signal that hints at how recovered you are.
- **RPE (rate of perceived exertion)**: how hard a set felt, on a simple scale.
- **Progressive overload**: adding a little weight or reps over time so you keep making progress.
- **Readiness**: a daily score for how recovered you are, built from your health data.
- **1RM (one-rep max)**: the most you could lift once. The app estimates it from your sets.

## Load-bearing facts

Copy must stay true to these. If a claim conflicts with the product, the claim is wrong.

- **Price**: €7.99/month, €69.99/year (save about 27%). iOS only, iOS 16+.
- **Exercise library**: "1,300+ exercises". This is a floor. Never state an exact
  number, and never a higher one.
- **Set types** are named, never counted: drop sets, supersets, myo-reps, giant sets.
  Clusters and AMRAP do not exist in the app. Never mention them.
- **Recovery** is "evidence-aligned", never "science-backed". It uses HRV, sleep and
  resting heart rate from Apple Health, scored into a daily readiness number.
- **Progress per exercise**: an estimated 1RM, max weight and volume, each as a chart
  over time, plus personal records for weight, estimated 1RM and volume. The estimate
  is the mean of published formulas (Epley, Brzycki and others). Name them, never count them.
- **Analysis** (the app's Analytics tab): Overview (next weight and reps per lift,
  recovery readiness, recent PRs); Balance (weekly volume by muscle group, push:pull
  ratio, performance drop); Progress (the per-exercise charts above, plateau help, effort
  (RPE), personal records).
- **Plateau detection**: per exercise, after at least six logged sessions, when top
  weight and work-set volume barely moved and the latest session did not beat the one
  before. The plan depends on effort: high effort, ease off the load (deload); low
  effort, train closer to failure or change the rep range. It is "a cue to look closer,
  not a verdict".
- **Progression** is "suggestions plus plateau detection", never "automatic". The app
  suggests when to add weight and when to hold back. It does not progress your lifts
  for you. Phrasing like "it suggests, it does not decide" is on-brand.

## Release gates

Do not write these until they are actually true. They are false or unsafe today.

- **No "no streaks" or "no gamification" claim.** The app currently still ships
  streaks and achievements. Do not claim otherwise.
- **No "free", "trial", or "30 days free".** Wait until Apple's introductory offer is
  configured and verified. A subscription call to action also needs a "renews
  automatically" line.
- **No competitor comparison table anywhere.** It is legally exposed, and the internal
  numbers are not publication-grade. Naming Strong or Hevy in prose on our own site is
  fine. A side-by-side table is not. The sanctioned line (from the app's brand rules) is
  "For lifters who have outgrown Strong and Hevy". Never state what Strong or Hevy cannot
  do. Show the difference through what Iron Forge does.
- **No launch date and no countdown.** A slipped date is exactly the broken promise the
  brand stands against. Before launch, the only commercial surface is the waitlist.

## The one check that matters most

Before any number or feature claim ships, check it against the product. A claim the
app cannot back is the failure mode, even when it sounds perfectly on-brand.
